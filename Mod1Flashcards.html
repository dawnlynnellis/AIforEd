✨ The 4 V's of Big Data Flashcards ✨

Click on any card to flip it and reveal the details.

Volume

Definition:

The **quantity** of data. Big Data is characterized by massive datasets—terabytes to petabytes and beyond—that are too large for traditional database systems.

Example:

The total data generated by **Google, Amazon, and Facebook** globally in a single day.

Analogy: **A Tsunami** (The sheer, overwhelming size and amount of water.)

Velocity

Definition:

The **speed** at which data is generated, collected, and must be processed, often in real-time or near real-time.

Example:

Tens of thousands of **financial market trades** or **social media posts** occurring and being processed every second.

Analogy: **A Race Car Driver** (The need for instantaneous reaction to changing track conditions.)

Variety

Definition:

The **diversity** of data formats, which includes structured (tables), semi-structured (JSON, XML), and unstructured (text, video, audio, logs).

Example:

Combining **customer purchase records** (structured) with their **social media comments** (unstructured) and **video feedback** (unstructured).

Analogy: **A Fruit Salad** (A mix of many different shapes, textures, and tastes of data.)

Veracity

Definition:

The **quality, reliability, and trustworthiness** of the data. Big Data often has inherent inconsistencies, incompleteness, or bias.

Example:

Trying to analyze **social media sentiment** when users use sarcasm, abbreviations, or post fake reviews.

Analogy: **A Rumor Mill** (Determining which information is true and which is just noise or misinformation.)
